AlpacaEval vs the v19 kernel

Lab book · 2026-11-11 · frozen v19 (sha256 1f63ee85…) through reading frame r1 · predictions frozen in PLAN.md before the first reading · observational, no new human data

Findings
  • Model answers changed style between 2023 and late 2024. Median length 210 → 291 tokens; answers with bold title lines 0% → 48% (median model); denser (ρᵣ), rarer vocabulary (xₚ), more segmented (rᵥ), less repetition, less connective cohesion (μ).
  • Models did not converge; they split. Spread between models grew from 1.05 to 1.75, also with length removed. In 2024-H2 the split partly follows the base model: every Gemma-2 and Llama-3.1/3.2 derivative (and Meta's own Llama-3.1-Instruct) uses bold title lines and sits far along the judge's preferred direction; every Infinity-Instruct, Qwen2 and Mistral entry stays plain; Llama-3.0 derivatives appear on both sides (spread 0.90 vs 1.56).
  • The judge rewards the same structure humans do, in the same direction. Judge and human weights on the 8 axes have cosine 0.92. Both penalise repetition equally (-0.338 vs -0.372). Within a prompt, the kernel adds +0.073 AUC to length for predicting the judge.
  • The length-controlled leaderboard still carries a structural style. Across 213 models, style explains LC win rate well beyond length (leave-one-out R² 0.25 → 0.69); a model's position along the judge's preferred direction has Spearman +0.84 with LC win rate. These data cannot separate style from quality.

1 · Data

227 models (NullModel excluded: it games the judge) × 805 instructions, 182,723 answers, 88.6% eligible after the reading frame. Style study: 218 models with ≥ 400 eligible answers and a submission date. Cohorts by the date the model's answers were committed to the AlpacaEval repo (not release date): 2023-H2 84, 2024-H1 98, 2024-H2 36. 2024-H2 is mostly community entries (30 of 36).

Judge: 151,754 verdicts on eligible pairs across 219 models; each verdict compares the model's answer with the fixed reference gpt4_1106_preview. The model's answer won 21.3% of them.

Added analysis H: the 2024-H2 split

Reading the 2024-H2 cohort model by model showed two groups; the 20% threshold was chosen after looking. The bold group is FuseChat, Gemma-2 (SimPO, WPO, DPO, SPPO, mixture-of-agents) and Llama-3.1/3.2 derivatives plus Meta's Llama-3.1-Instruct; the plain group is the Infinity-Instruct series, Qwen2, Mistral, gpt-4o-mini, higgs-llama-3 and Llama-3.0 preference-tuned models (WPO-HB-v2, PBM-Nova, blendaxai-l3), several of which also score high. Llama-3.0 derivatives also appear in the bold group (GPO, SPPO, REBEL, RainbowPO, SkillMix). So the split partly tracks the base model each entry was built on; for Llama-3.0 the fine-tune decides. Models with bold title lines in ≥ 20% of answers: n=23, median LC win rate 54.0%. The rest: n=13, median 38.0%. Spread within the plain group (0.90) is lower than 2023-H2 overall (1.06); no interval, post hoc.

Same features, standardised within each dataset. The two patterns point the same way (cosine 0.92). The judge's weights are larger, but AlpacaEval pairs (any model vs one strong reference) span a much wider range than HelpSteer2 pairs, and the human labels are 5-point ratings, so sizes are not comparable across the two datasets. 5 · What this does and does not show

  • Observational throughout. Cohort dates are commit dates; the 2024-H2 cohort is mostly community submissions to this leaderboard.
  • The structure the judge rewards is the structure humans reward, by direction. Whether the judge over-rewards it cannot be settled with these two datasets.
  • Style and quality move together at model level; these data cannot pull them apart.
  • Many of the 2024-H2 leaders inherit a structural signature from their base models (Gemma-2, Llama-3.1): bold titles, density, rare vocabulary. The length control does not remove it. Base model and leaderboard success are entangled here.
